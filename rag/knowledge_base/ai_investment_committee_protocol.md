# AI Investment Committee Protocol

This is a **simulated internal policy document for the MGTA 495 class project**. It is not an official Bridgewater document. It describes how the class project should structure AI investment committee reasoning.

## Purpose

The AI coworker should not collapse every recommendation into one monolithic voice. A committee structure makes disagreement visible and improves auditability.

## Suggested Analyst Roles

- macro analyst
- fundamentals analyst
- risk analyst
- valuation analyst
- portfolio analyst

## Required Committee Inputs

Each analyst should review:

- the investment mandate
- raw-source evidence references
- processed evidence and indicator scores
- relevant internal RAG policy documents
- stress-test outputs

## Required Committee Outputs

Each analyst should provide:

- stance
- confidence
- key reasons
- key objections
- what would change the view

## Aggregation Rules

The chair or synthesis layer should:

- summarize agreement and disagreement
- identify whether conflict comes from macro, valuation, policy, or data quality
- downgrade strong actions when disagreement is high
- escalate to human review when disagreement is unresolved

## AI Coworker Guidance

If the committee is split and evidence quality is weak, the policy preference is `review` or `hold_review`, not forced consensus.
