# Thesis Monitoring Guidelines

## Purpose
Use this guide to determine whether new evidence supports, weakens, or threatens a company thesis. The task is not generic sentiment classification. The task is to map evidence to explicit thesis assumptions and decide whether the thesis still deserves the same confidence.

## Definitions

### Supporting
Evidence is supporting when it reinforces one or more key assumptions without introducing a material offsetting concern.

### Neutral
Evidence is neutral when it is informational but does not materially change the thesis path, confidence, or monitoring priority.

### Weakening
Evidence is weakening when it raises doubt about a key assumption, slows the expected timeline, narrows upside, or increases dependence on fewer favorable conditions.

### Thesis-breaking
Evidence is thesis-breaking when it directly invalidates a central assumption or reveals that expected economics, market access, or strategic positioning are no longer credible under the original thesis logic.

## Required Process

1. Read the original thesis before reading the evidence.
2. List the thesis assumptions in plain language.
3. For each news item or evidence item, identify which assumption it touches.
4. Classify the item relative to that assumption, not relative to headline tone.
5. Note whether the evidence changes:
- magnitude
- timing
- confidence
- reversibility

## Decision Rules

### Classify as supporting when
- the evidence confirms adoption, demand, economics, market access, or execution that the thesis depends on
- the evidence broadens the set of customers or use cases supporting the thesis
- the evidence reduces a previously important uncertainty

### Classify as weakening when
- the evidence makes success more conditional
- the evidence increases concentration, timing, policy, or execution risk
- the evidence suggests that upside is still possible but less robust than before

### Classify as thesis-breaking when
- a central market, product path, or economic engine is no longer viable under reasonable assumptions
- a core assumption fails and there is no plausible offset large enough to preserve the same thesis structure
- multiple independent evidence items point to the same assumption failure

## Handling Conflicting Evidence
Conflicting evidence should be expected in live monitoring. Do not collapse everything into one score too early.

When evidence conflicts:
1. Separate demand evidence from supply evidence.
2. Separate operational evidence from valuation evidence.
3. Rank evidence by thesis relevance, not by volume.
4. Prefer direct company-impact evidence over broad market commentary.
5. Keep the thesis intact if the conflict affects timing more than ultimate economics, unless the thesis was explicitly timing-dependent.

## Weighting Rules
Weight evidence more heavily when it is:
- directly connected to a key assumption
- recent
- repeated across independent sources
- difficult to reverse
- likely to affect multiple downstream metrics

Weight evidence less heavily when it is:
- speculative
- based on broad market chatter
- not clearly linked to the thesis assumptions
- duplicative of already-known information

## Expected Output
For each evidence item, produce:
- assumption affected
- classification
- one-sentence reasoning
- confidence level

For the aggregate thesis monitor, produce:
- assumptions reinforced
- assumptions under pressure
- open questions
- whether escalation is needed

## Human Review Triggers
Escalate when:
- the classification depends on ambiguous policy or legal interpretation
- two or more thesis-breaking signals appear in independent evidence streams
- the thesis can only be preserved through a major rewrite rather than a modest update
- evidence changes position-sizing logic even if the long-term narrative is still arguable

The standard is structured judgment. Avoid both naive optimism and overreaction to single headlines.
