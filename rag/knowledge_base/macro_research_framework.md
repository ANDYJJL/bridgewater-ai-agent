# Macro Research Framework

## Purpose
Use this framework to convert macro evidence into company-relevant research context. The goal is not to restate macro data. The goal is to decide which macro forces matter, through what channel they matter, and whether they change the expected path of the company thesis.

## Core Principle
Macro matters through transmission channels, not through isolated statistics. A higher rate print, lower unemployment print, or softer inflation print is not itself a thesis update. The model must explain how the move travels through customer budgets, valuation sensitivity, credit conditions, trade flows, or input costs.

## Definitions

### First-order macro effects
Effects that directly change revenue timing, demand formation, financing conditions, or cost structure.

Examples:
- changes in enterprise IT budgets
- changes in customer capital expenditure appetite
- changes in financing cost for duration-sensitive assets
- changes in export conditions or trade access

### Second-order macro effects
Effects that influence the first-order drivers indirectly.

Examples:
- softer growth leading to more budget discipline
- disinflation changing policy expectations and valuation multiples
- tighter labor markets affecting wage costs or implementation speed

### Regime change
A regime change occurs when the direction of macro evidence is broad enough, durable enough, and economically important enough that base assumptions should be revisited. A single data release rarely qualifies. Look for confirmation across related variables.

## What to Look For

### Growth
Assess whether activity is accelerating, decelerating, or merely noisy. Growth matters differently by business model:
- cyclical businesses depend on broad demand
- platform businesses depend on customer investment appetite
- premium-growth equities are often sensitive to the durability of long-duration expectations

### Inflation and rates
Treat inflation and rates as both operational and valuation variables.
- Operational channel: input costs, customer pricing behavior, wage pressure
- Valuation channel: discount rates, multiple compression, hurdle rates for large projects

### Labor
Labor data often matters indirectly. A strong labor market can support spending and investment confidence. A soft labor market can signal tighter budgets, slower deployment, or a less favorable demand backdrop.

### Policy and trade
Policy variables can dominate the cycle when they alter market access, cost structure, or compliance obligations. When policy shocks conflict with otherwise constructive demand data, do not average them away. Explicitly model the conflict.

## Decision Rules

1. Start by selecting only the macro variables that plausibly affect the target company.
2. For each variable, state the transmission path in one sentence.
3. Distinguish current condition from forward implication.
4. Look for confirmation across at least two related indicators before calling a regime shift.
5. If the macro picture is mixed, identify which variable is most thesis-relevant instead of forcing a single directional macro label.

## Handling Conflicting Evidence
Conflicting evidence is normal. Do not resolve it by simple averaging. Resolve it by asking:
- which variable has the shortest transmission path to the company?
- which variable changes customer behavior most directly?
- which variable has the strongest historical impact on valuation or revenue timing?

If one variable affects demand while another affects valuation, keep both channels separate in the output.

## Expected Output Structure
The macro analysis should produce:
- the current macro backdrop
- the 2 to 4 most thesis-relevant variables
- whether each variable is tailwind, headwind, or monitor-only
- the likely transmission channel
- what downstream scenario design should stress

## Escalation Guidance
Escalate to human review when:
- policy and macro signals point in opposite directions with large consequences
- data quality is inconsistent across key series
- the company thesis depends on a narrow macro assumption that is no longer well supported
- the macro change would require re-underwriting valuation, not just updating the narrative

The research standard is disciplined interpretation, not theatrical certainty.
